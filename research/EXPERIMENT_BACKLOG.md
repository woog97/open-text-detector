## Priority scale and decision method

- **5/5 — Immediate:** a cheap decision-critical check or a direct intervention against an observed paper-detection weakness. Prepare/run next when dependencies permit.
- **4/5 — High:** plausible substantial benefit, but an unresolved data, label, or transfer assumption remains. One controlled pilot, not a sweep.
- **3/5 — Conditional:** useful after specified results; not a reason to occupy a GPU immediately.
- **2/5 — Defer:** uncertain paper benefit, substantial preparation/cost, or a cheaper experiment answers the question first.
- **1/5 — Do not pursue now:** redundant, confounded, or poorly matched to our goal.

Within each band, rank by (1) relevance to paper token/sentence recall at low human FPR, (2) evidence of the targeted failure, (3) ability to distinguish competing explanations, (4) preparation and GPU cost, and (5) whether the result enables later decisions. Do not sum invented numerical estimates into a spurious precise ROI score. Confidence below means confidence in the experiment's usefulness, not that it will improve the model.

Preparation and training have separate priorities: preparing an audited dataset can be worthwhile while its training pilot waits. A conditional confirmation can outrank an immediately runnable experiment without becoming runnable first. Idle GPU time alone does not justify a low-value job.

## Evidence driving this ranking

- Fresh paper control / RAID / MAGE target token F1: **89.56 / 80.68 / 81.32%**; recall **82.44 / 68.11 / 69.32%**; human token FPR **1.56 / 0.70 / 1.10%**. These are different realized operating points, so neither superiority nor inferior discrimination follows from F1 alone. Broad gains do not establish a paper gain. RAID also worsened untouched-paper and ELLIPSE false positives. [Results](../benchmarks/otd/training/diversity-sweep/RESULTS.md).
- Existing sampling had only **18–25 short-AI examples per 12,000-draw epoch**. That establishes a coverage gap, not proof that more such examples will help.
- Increasing the untouched-human share helped some FPR measures but hurt reconstruction recall. Targeting particular human failures is more informative than another global ratio sweep.
- The score audit found both threshold mismatch and limited separation. Exact original batching reproduced saved scores; repeating the same corruption investigation has no remaining priority.
- Broad mixture, generator variety, source quality, and domain effects are confounded in our first public-data pilots. More datasets are available, but more is not automatically better.

## Recommended preparation order and decision gates

1. **Now, in parallel with current runs:** prepare the selection/operating-point analysis and training-only hard-human pool. Begin source/label audits for SeqXGPT and Arena without claiming they are ready training pools.
2. **When boundary/curriculum results arrive:** examine paper selection tradeoffs, major workflow slices and human FPR. If a candidate helps, confirm it with a matched seed; if not, avoid combinations and large-backbone transfer.
3. **Next independent training hypotheses:** hard-human pilot, mixed-authorship donor pilot, then Arena pilot when its splits/human controls are ready. Run the objective ablation if these fail or if loss/selection behavior points to conflicting supervision.
4. **Only after a robust gain:** combine complementary changes or transfer to Qwen3.5. Use remaining public pools to address observed residual errors, not to maximize dataset count.

Before each pilot, freeze one primary paper recall/FPR selection criterion and a minimum useful change, with token/sentence F1, precision, full-paragraph recall, untouched-human FPR and subgroup results as guardrails. Use paper-group uncertainty. If no FPR target has been formally adopted, report a predeclared low-FPR curve (e.g. 0.5%, 1%, 2%) rather than silently deciding that 1% is acceptable. Calibration uncertainty matters; small human pools cannot establish very low FPR reliably. Do not tune thresholds on test labels.

Maintain training/model-selection/calibration/test separation. Many existing tests have informed hypotheses and are now research-exposed; seed replication does not restore a blind test. Reserve untouched source groups for confirmation. Document-only labels never become exact span gold by assumption.

    What counts as a clause

    A clause is a group of words with its own subject and verb. The blue cuts are a draft; fix them so each tinted run is one clause.

    Cut before an independent clause (after and, but, ;), a subordinate clause (because, although, when, if), a relative clause (which, that, who), and a that-complement of a verb like show or argue.

    We train on ImageNet /because it is large, /which makes results comparable.

    Don't cut phrases with no subject and verb of their own: participle and infinitive phrases, prepositional phrases, citations, parentheticals, equations, list items without a verb.

    The bound specified in the algorithm holds for any K (see Section 3) [12].

    A clause inside another clause: cut where it starts and again where the outer clause picks up. The leftover piece can look like a fragment; that's expected.

    The guarantees /that the chain converges /vanish.

    When a sentence is genuinely unclear, mark your best guess and press Save as unsure. Stop whenever you like; every saved sentence counts.

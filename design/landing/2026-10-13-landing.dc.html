Check an ICLR 2027 submission for AI-written text

This site runs an open-source classifier I trained to detect LLM writing in ML papers. I ran it on all 42,000~ish ICLR 2027 submissions and host the sentence-level classification results here with an integrated pdf viewer. It is free and independent, with no company or commercial funding behind it. Why I built it.

This is not a slop detector

The classifier measures whether writing resembles text generated or edited by a language model. It does not measure a paper’s quality, whether its ideas came from a human or model, or whether its reasoning holds up. A paper with many flagged sentences is not, on that evidence alone, a worse paper.

When making this project originally, I was hoping that the proportion of AI aided writing in a paper would be highly correlated to its likelihood of being slop. The jury is still out on whether this is true, but I don't have enough evidence to make this claim yet. What I do have evidence of, is that the classifier assigns substantially higher confidence scores on held-out eval sets I designed to imitate slop (I just don't trust the evals that much). See out the ROC curves in the calibration section for details. 

The site offers a few heuristics along these lines, such as the share of a paper’s sentences above a threshold, and sorting submissions by that share. You can choose which human false positive rate to use and control the sensitivity of the classifier. I invite you to take a look at the ROC curves, 

A score is a model estimate, not proof of misconduct, and the detector makes errors. Read the highlighted passages before drawing any conclusion.

Why this exists

ICLR runs its review in the open. Every submission, review and rebuttal is public while the process is under way. That openness makes it possible to look at how papers are written, not only what they claim.

Undisclosed use of language models in writing is hard to see and slow to address. Conferences ask for disclosure but have few ways to check it while review is still happening. This site puts the same evidence in front of everyone at the same time, so that authors, reviewers and chairs can discuss it with the paper in hand.

Who made this

Built by woog, self-funded and unpaid, with help from a collaborator, sky.moo, and donated compute from a Hugging Face community grant (through zmuwztd) and nev. I curated and produced the training data, trained the detectors and evaluated them. Commercial detectors keep their models and data closed. This one is meant to be fully open: the results are on this site, and the training recipe, a full dataset disclosure and the LoRA adapters will be published.

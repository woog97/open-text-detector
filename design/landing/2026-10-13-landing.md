## Why

The old "How to use it" section assumed the scores work as a slop detector. They don't, and the page must say so plainly: the classifier measures resemblance to AI-generated or AI-edited text, not paper quality. The section order puts this right after the lookup box because most visitors read only the first section.

## Changes

Make exactly these changes; leave everything else in `Home()` and the site chrome as it is.

1. **Intro paragraph** (first `<p>` under the `<h1>`). Replace its text with this, keeping the existing "Why I built it." link at the end:

   > This site runs an open-source classifier I trained to detect LLM writing in ML papers. I ran it on all 42,000~ish ICLR 2027 submissions and host the sentence-level classification results here with an integrated pdf viewer. It is free and independent, with no company or commercial funding behind it. Why I built it.

2. **Meta line** under the search form. Remove `scores are provisional until launch · ` so it reads `ICLR 2027 submissions · Browse all submissions`.

3. **New section "This is not a slop detector"**, inserted directly after the hero section and before "Why this exists". Use the same `<Section>` component as the other sections. Three paragraphs, verbatim (Alice merged the old fourth paragraph into the third on Oct 13):

   > The classifier measures whether writing resembles text generated or edited by a language model. It does not measure a paper’s quality, whether its ideas came from a human or model, or whether its reasoning holds up. A paper with many flagged sentences is not, on that evidence alone, a worse paper.

   > When making this project originally, I was hoping that the proportion of AI aided writing in a paper would be highly correlated to its likelihood of being slop. The jury is still out on whether this is true, but I don't have enough evidence to make this claim yet. What I do have evidence of, is that the classifier assigns substantially higher confidence scores on held-out eval sets I designed to imitate slop (I just don't trust the evals that much). See out the ROC curves in the calibration section for details.

   > The site offers a few heuristics along these lines, such as the share of a paper’s sentences above a threshold, and sorting submissions by that share. You can choose which human false positive rate to use and control the sensitivity of the classifier. I set the default human FPR to 0.1%, but you can change it to 0.01% to try catching more egregious forms of slop. Mandatory reminder: a score is a model estimate, not proof of misconduct, and the detector makes errors. Read the highlighted passages before drawing any conclusion.

   Link "calibration section" in paragraph 2 to `/?view=calibration`, like other internal links on the page.
